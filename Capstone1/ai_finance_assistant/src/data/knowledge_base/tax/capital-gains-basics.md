When you sell an investment such as a stock or bond, your capital gain or loss is the difference between what you received and your adjusted basis, which is generally what the asset cost you. Selling for more than your basis creates a gain, and selling for less creates a loss. Losses on personal-use property, such as your home or car, are not tax deductible.

## Short-term versus long-term
Holding an asset for more than one year generally makes the gain or loss long-term. Holding it for one year or less makes it short-term. To count the holding period, start the day after you acquired the asset and include the day you sold it. Some assets, such as property received as a gift or inheritance, follow different rules.

## Tax rates for 2025
Net capital gain may be taxed at a lower rate than ordinary income. For 2025, a 0% rate applies if taxable income is at or below $48,350 for single filers, $96,700 for married couples filing jointly, or $64,750 for heads of household. A 15% rate applies above those levels up to $533,400 for single filers, $600,050 for joint filers, and $566,700 for heads of household. A 20% rate applies above that. Certain gains, such as those from collectibles, can be taxed at up to 28%. These thresholds change each year.
